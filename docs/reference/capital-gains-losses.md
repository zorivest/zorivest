# Capital Gains and Losses

## What Are They?

A **capital gain** or **capital loss** is the profit or loss from selling a capital asset (stocks, bonds, real estate, etc.) for more or less than you paid.

---

## Classification

### By Holding Period

| Holding Period | Classification | Tax Rate |
|---------------|---------------|----------|
| ≤ 1 year (365 days) | **Short-term** | Ordinary income rates (10%–37%) |
| > 1 year | **Long-term** | Preferential rates (0%, 15%, or 20%) |

### Net Capital Gain/Loss

At the end of the year, short-term and long-term gains/losses are netted separately, then combined:

1. Net short-term gains against short-term losses.
2. Net long-term gains against long-term losses.
3. If one is a net gain and the other a net loss, they offset each other.

---

## Capital Loss Rules

- **Deduction limit**: Up to $3,000 in net capital losses can be deducted against ordinary income per year ($1,500 if married filing separately).
- **Carryforward**: Excess losses carry forward indefinitely to future tax years.
- **No carryback**: Capital losses cannot be applied to prior years' income.

---

## Tax-Loss Harvesting

A strategy of intentionally selling losing positions to realize losses that offset gains:

1. Sell a position with an unrealized loss.
2. Use the realized loss to offset capital gains.
3. Optionally, repurchase a similar (but not substantially identical) security.

⚠️ **Watch for wash sales** — repurchasing the same security within 30 days disallows the loss. See wash-sales.md.
